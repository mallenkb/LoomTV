Issues to address first
1

Metadata recovery is missing from the action model.

Information architectureHigh-priority issue

Evidence. The visible controls offer “Fix Match,” artwork changes, and “Reveal in Finder,” but no action to refresh missing episode titles, descriptions, dates, ratings, providers, or character credits.

Problem. The user’s recovery goal—fill incomplete metadata—has no recognizable, scoped entry point.

Why it matters. Users must infer that an artwork or match action may help, then still cannot deliberately repair partial records. This creates repeated support requests and makes incomplete data appear permanent.

Quick win. Add “Refresh metadata” beside this action cluster and in Details. Scope it to missing fields only; show progress, a result summary, and preserve populated values. Validate through successful repair of a missing episode description and character portrait.
2

“Fix Match” is too broad for the actions around it.

CopyMedium-priority issue

Evidence. The primary control reads “Fix Match,” while the adjacent overflow lists “Fix cover,” “Upload poster,” “Upload cover,” and “Reveal in Finder.”

Problem. “Match” could mean title identity, artwork, or metadata. The menu itself then mixes a repair verb with manual file and artwork actions.

Why it matters. Users cannot predict which control will repair the information they see as wrong.

Quick win. Rename the primary action to “Match title” and add “Refresh metadata.” Group the overflow under “Artwork” and “File” labels. Validate with a five-user task: “refresh the missing episode description.”
3

Poster and cover terminology needs an explicit distinction.

Mental modelMedium-priority issue

Evidence. The menu presents “Fix cover,” “Upload poster,” and “Upload cover” as adjacent actions.

Problem. The screen does not explain whether “poster” is the vertical tile image and “cover” is the wide hero backdrop, or whether “Fix cover” searches while “Upload cover” replaces it.

Why it matters. Users may alter the wrong asset or not understand the difference between an automatic repair and a manual upload.

Quick win. Use “Fix backdrop” / “Upload backdrop” if the asset is the hero image, and add concise helper text in the menu or a dialog. Validate by asking users which action changes the hero background.
Needs verification
4

The overflow control’s accessible contract is not visible.

AccessibilityNeeds verification

Evidence. The three-dot trigger is icon-only; the open menu has large rows, but focus and announcement behavior are not visible.

Risk. The trigger may be understandable visually, yet its accessible name, keyboard opening, Escape behavior, focus return, and screen-reader menu semantics cannot be confirmed from this screenshot.

Why verify. This menu contains recovery actions; users need a predictable way to open, navigate, and exit it without a pointer.

Recommendation. Verify an accessible name such as “More title actions,” menu-button semantics, arrow-key navigation, Escape/return focus, visible focus, and 44×44 px targets.
Working well to preserve
5

Local file recovery is easy to discover.

User controlWorking well

Evidence. “Reveal in Finder” is a plainly named, separated option at the bottom of the menu.

Why it works. It gives users a direct, familiar escape hatch to inspect the local asset without overloading the title page.

What to preserve. Keep this command visible and separate it from metadata and artwork actions.

and metadata recovery.

The shortest path to 9.0 is not more menu items. It is a clear action model: match the title, refresh missing metadata, manage artwork, and inspect the local file. Keep refresh field-safe and communicate what changed.
